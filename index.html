Christina Wang

Lederman Fellow, Fermilab · Batavia, Illinois

I am a particle physicist working at the intersection of high energy physics and quantum sensing. My research focuses on searches for unconventional signatures at the CMS experiment, developing superconducting nanowire sensors for high energy particle and dark matter detection, and precision timing detectors for the HL-LHC upgrade.

I am currently a Lederman Fellow at Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab), Batavia, Illinois. I completed my Ph.D. in Physics at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) in 2024 and my B.S. in Physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) in 2018.

Research

See publications & public results

Unconventional Searchs at the LHC

Searches for particles that travel a measurable distance before decaying — including muon detector showers (MDS), heavy stable charged particles (HSCP), and delayed & trackless jets. Discovered the MDS signature and built its Run 3 trigger, improving efficiency by an order of magnitude.

Superconducting nanowire detectors

Builds and characterizes large-area SNSPDs, measuring a record-low dark count rate of 10⁻³ Hz and performing the first characterization of SNSPD response to high-energy particles at Fermilab and CERN test beams.

Light dark-matter searches

Couples SNSPDs to a parabolic reflector (BREAD) for axion searches in the meV–eV mass range, and to a GaAs cryogenic scintillator to search for sub-GeV dark matter.

Precision timing

Characterizes LYSO crystal and SiPM geometries and tests ASIC readout for the CMS MIP Timing Detector upgrade, at test beams from Fermilab to Los Alamos.
