About

Christina Wang is a Lederman Fellow at Fermilab, where her work spans two fronts: searching for long-lived particles (LLPs) and other exotic signatures with the CMS experiment at the LHC, and developing superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) for dark matter and quantum sensing applications.

Research

flagship research area

CMS Long-lived particles

Searches for particles that travel a measurable distance through the CMS detector before decaying, across several complementary final states.

Muon detector showers (MDS)

Discovered the MDS signature and built its reconstruction, identification, and Run 3 trigger — improving trigger efficiency by an order of magnitude (CMS-DP-2023-043, CMS-DP-2022-062) — plus a Delphes fast-simulation module enabling reinterpretation across LLP models.

Heavy stable charged particles (HSCP)

Searches for highly ionizing long-lived charged particles, setting limits on gluino masses up to 2 TeV and tau slepton masses up to 0.69 TeV.

Delayed & trackless jets

Best sensitivity for long-lived neutralinos with decay lengths of 0.3–1.0 m, excluding masses up to 1.18 TeV.

quantum sensing

Superconducting nanowire detectors

Builds and characterizes large-area SNSPDs, measuring a record-low dark count rate of 10⁻³ Hz and performing the first characterization of SNSPD response to high-energy particles at Fermilab and CERN test beams.

axion & dark matter

Light dark-matter searches

Couples SNSPDs to a parabolic reflector (BREAD) for axion searches in the meV–eV mass range, and to a GaAs cryogenic scintillator to search for sub-GeV dark matter.

detector R&D

Precision timing for the HL-LHC

Characterizes LYSO crystal and SiPM geometries and tests ASIC readout for the CMS MIP Timing Detector upgrade, at test beams from Fermilab to Los Alamos.
